CABINET NO. 20 — 1983

FİŞEK

One shell a round, and it does not go off until you let go of it. Everything it catches bursts in turn and each one in the chain pays more than the last, so the whole cabinet is a single decision made once every few seconds: fire now, or wait for the drift to bring one more into the ring.

CABINET NO. 21 — 1982

BOYA

The only cabinet here where you take ground instead of surviving it. Leave the paint and the brush drags a wet line behind it; bring it back to colour and everything you closed off floods in. Every sheet is a new colour, so you can see how far you got — and an ink blot that touches a line still wet costs you the page.

Hold to paint · Arrows ▶ PLAY
CABINET NO. 22 — 1979

RAY

The strategy cabinet. There is no brake and no steering — you lay the rail ahead of the train, left, ahead or right, and it takes whatever line you built. Ties arrive one at a time from the depot, so a long straight burns them faster than they come; straight track is fast and pays double, and every bend hands the speed back. Run out of rail and you derail.

Three buttons · Arrows ▶ PLAY
CABINET NO. 23 — 1985

FENER

The only cabinet here that hides anything from you. A minefield crossed on foot at night: stand on a square and it tells you how many mines touch it, and you work out the rest. You are crossing, not clearing, so a corner you cannot solve can be walked around. No field ever makes you guess — every one is thrown away unless a solver can cross it — so a mine is always a choice you made. The lamp is the clock, and the oil is always lying next to a mine.

D-pad to walk · Tap to mark ▶ PLAY
CABINET NO. 24 — 1988

TAKLA

One button, and the whole game is in it. Hold to spin, let go and the rider straightens up — which takes time you may not have left. You keep the flips you more than half finished. Every ramp is signed with the trick it will give you and the sign never lies, but a gold sign means there is one more in it if you can read the air. The speed you win from flips is the speed that clears the next gap, so a bail costs you both.

Hold anywhere · Space ▶ PLAY
CABINET NO. 25 — 1983

İKİZ

Every other cabinet here gives you one thing to control. This one gives you two, and one input for both — going left for one twin sends the other right. Catch the fruit on either side, dodge the bombs on both. A safe lane always exists and no bomb is ever allowed into it; the fruit is what pulls you out. The hard part is not your thumb, it is that you cannot watch both halves at once.

Drag the rail · Arrows ▶ PLAY
CABINET NO. 26 — 1986

GÖLGE

FENER hides the room from you; this hides you from the guards. Hold a side to walk it, let go to stand still — and a beam fills the alarm faster than the dark empties it, so holding a button and walking gets you caught. The corridor keeps splitting in two and each branch has its own lamp. Neither side is the safe one: which branch is clear depends on when you get there, and picking wrong costs you half the bar.

Hold anywhere · Space ▶ PLAY
CABINET NO. 27 — 1987

NABIZ

Four drums, four speeds, and only one strike per beat between them. Sooner or later two come due on the same beat and you cannot serve both — so you clear one early, which costs you almost nothing and pays you almost nothing. Waiting for the line pays the drum's whole count. Every pattern can be played clean, and the cabinet checks that before it deals one.
